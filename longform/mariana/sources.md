# Mariana Trench — fact sources

- Plastic bag + candy wrappers at the bottom, Vescovo 2019 dive (10,927 m, Five Deeps): AP via WTOP, 13 May 2019 — https://wtop.com/science/2019/05/record-breaking-sea-dive-finds-plastic-bag-in-worlds-deepest-trench/
- Subduction drags ~3× more water into the Earth (Mariana ~4×), 19 ocean-bottom + 7 island seismometers, >1 year, hydrated layer ~20 miles (~30 km): Washington University in St. Louis, Nov 2018 (Cai et al., Nature) — https://source.washu.edu/2018/11/seismic-study-reveals-huge-amount-of-water-dragged-into-earths-interior/
- Deepest fish filmed, Pseudoliparis snailfish at 8,336 m, Izu-Ogasawara Trench, Aug 2022 (announced Apr 2023), Minderoo-UWA / Alan Jamieson: NPR, 5 Apr 2023 — https://www.npr.org/2023/04/05/1168051498/deepest-fish-japan-photograph
- Champagne vent, NW Eifuku (~1,600 m), liquid CO₂ droplets (~98% CO₂, <4 °C): NOAA PMEL, Lupton et al. 2006 — https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/pubs/outstand/lupt2843/abstract.shtml
- Microplastics in amphipods of six deep trenches; 100% of Mariana samples: Jamieson et al. 2019, Royal Society Open Science (via National Geographic) — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/deep-sea-creatures-mariana-trench-eat-plastic
- Trieste dive, 23 Jan 1960, Piccard & Walsh, outer window cracked ~9,000 m, ~20 min on the bottom: U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings, "First Deepest Dive", Jan 2020 — https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2020/january/first-deepest-dive
- Common reference values: Challenger Deep ~10,935 m; Everest 8,849 m; trench ~2,550 km long; ~1,071 atm at the bottom (≈15,750 psi ≈ 7.1 t per square inch); xenophyophores found at ~10.6 km (Scripps 2011).
