# Russia — pilot script v1 (hook + chapter 1)

Format: `N:` narrator · `TRAVELLER:` our time-traveller mascot · other speakers are skit characters. `*word*` = keyword caption. `[tag]` = delivery. ~1,050 words ≈ 7 min.
Facts marked † are legends from the Primary Chronicle and are framed as "the story goes".

---

# HOOK

N: In eighteen sixty-seven, Russia sold a piece of land bigger than *Germany, France and Spain combined*.
N: The price? About *two cents* an acre.
N: The buyer was America. The land was *Alaska*. And not long after, people found gold there. Then oil.
TRAVELLER: [checking his pocket watch, wincing] Oof. Worst garage sale in history.
N: But here's the weird part. Russia barely missed it. Because even *without* Alaska, Russia is still the biggest country on Earth.
N: It covers *eleven time zones*. When Moscow is brushing its teeth before bed, the far east of Russia is already eating tomorrow's breakfast.
N: And over eleven hundred years, this land has been run by Viking chiefs, Mongol khans, a tsar who killed his own son, a giant who shaved his nobles' beards by force, a German princess who took her husband's throne, and a dictator who erased people from photos.
TRAVELLER: [nervous] I've been to their family dinners. Bring a helmet.
N: But look closely, and Russia keeps living *the same story*.
N: First, *chaos*. Then one man grabs *total control*. People trade their freedom for order. Then that control goes *too far*, everything *collapses*... and the story starts again.
N: Vikings. Mongols. Tsars. Communists. Different hats. *Same story.*
N: So how did a bunch of feuding forest tribes build the largest country in history? And why can't Russia escape that cycle?
TRAVELLER: [winding his watch] Hold on to something.
N: Let's go back to the very beginning.

# CHAPTER 1: THE VIKINGS

N: About twelve hundred years ago, there was no Russia. Just forest, swamp... and *rivers*. So many rivers.
N: Along them lived tribes called the *East Slavs*. They farmed, they hunted, they traded fur. And they had one big problem.
N: They could not. Stop. *Fighting*.
N: So, the story goes†, they did something no one expected. They asked *foreigners* to come and rule them.
N: And not just any foreigners. *Vikings*.

[SKIT: log cabin, three Slav elders around a table, one writing on birch bark]
ELDER 1: [dictating] "Dear Vikings. Our land is huge and rich... but nobody is in charge."
ELDER 2: [horrified] The Vikings? The ones who raid monasteries for fun?
ELDER 1: You have a better plan?
ELDER 3: We could... stop fighting each other?
ELDER 1 & 2: [laughing] Hah! Good one.
ELDER 1: Sign it.

TRAVELLER: [to camera] Historians aren't sure that letter ever happened. But it's a great story.
N: According to Russia's oldest history book, in *eight sixty-two*, a Viking named *Rurik* arrived and took charge of the trading town of *Novgorod*.
N: The locals called these Vikings the *Rus*. And that is where the name *Russia* comes from.
N: Rurik's family would rule this land for more than *seven hundred years*.
N: After Rurik, a Viking named *Oleg* sailed south down the river and grabbed a rich town on the Dnieper: *Kyiv*.
N: Kyiv sat on the trade road between the Baltic Sea and the richest city on Earth, *Constantinople*. Furs and honey went south. Silver and silk came north.
N: Their new state is called *Kievan Rus*. Chaos had produced... one ruling family. *Order.*
TRAVELLER: [making a note] Loop step one. Check.

N: Then came a ruler with the scariest revenge story in Russian history: *Princess Olga*.
N: In nine forty-five, a tribe called the *Drevlians* killed her husband, Prince Igor, for demanding too much tax.
N: Then they sent men to propose that Olga marry *their* prince.
[SKIT: river dock, Drevlian envoys bowing in a boat, Olga smiling]
DREVLIAN: Great Princess! Our prince would like to marry you!
OLGA: [sweetly] How lovely. Come. I'll give you a *great* welcome.
N: The story goes† she had the first group buried alive, still in their boat. The second group was invited to a nice warm *bathhouse*... and the doors were locked.
TRAVELLER: [hiding behind his watch] Never accept a spa invitation in the tenth century.
N: And then, legend says, she burned the Drevlians' capital to the ground.
N: Strangely, the church later made her a *saint*.
TRAVELLER: A saint? Bold choice.

N: Her grandson *Vladimir* made the decision that still shapes Russia today: its *religion*.
N: The story goes† he interviewed religions like job candidates.
[SKIT: palace hall, Vladimir on a throne, three envoys lined up]
VLADIMIR: [shivering] No alcohol? In *this* winter? [shakes head] Next.
ENVOY: [whispering] Sire... the churches in Constantinople are *beautiful*.
VLADIMIR: Now we're talking.
N: In about *nine eighty-eight*, Vladimir chose *Orthodox Christianity*, and had the people of Kyiv baptized in the river.
N: Kievan Rus was now rich, Christian and connected to the greatest city in the world. Under his son *Yaroslav the Wise*, it was one of the biggest states in Europe.
N: And then... the family did what this family always did.
TRAVELLER: [sighing] They started fighting each other.
N: Brothers fought brothers. Cousins fought cousins. The state split into a dozen squabbling princedoms.
N: *Chaos*, again.
N: And while the princes were busy fighting each other... far to the east, the most terrifying army in human history had just started riding *west*.
TRAVELLER: [watch alarm ringing, eyes wide] Oh no. It's *them*.
