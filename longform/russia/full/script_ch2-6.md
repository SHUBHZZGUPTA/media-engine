# Russia — script, chapters 2–6 (first half, for review)

Same format as the pilot: `N:` narrator · `TRAVELLER:` mascot · other names are skit characters. `*word*` = keyword caption. `[tag]` = delivery.
Legends and disputed details are framed as "the story goes" / "legend says". ~3,000 words ≈ 21 min at the sped-up pace.

---

# CHAPTER 2: THE MONGOLS

N: The first time the Rus met the Mongols was in *twelve twenty-three*, at a river called the *Kalka*.
N: The princes of Kievan Rus rode out together for once. And it went... very badly.
TRAVELLER: [wincing] Their first team-building exercise. Total disaster.
N: Then the Mongols vanished back into the steppe. For fourteen years, nothing.
N: And in *twelve thirty-seven*, they came back. This time, led by *Batu Khan*, grandson of *Genghis Khan*.
N: City after city fell. *Ryazan*. *Vladimir*. In *twelve forty*, the jewel of the whole land, *Kyiv*, was burned to the ground.
TRAVELLER: [quietly] Remember Kyiv? The rich city on the trade road? Gone.
N: The Mongols didn't stay to rule the forests themselves. They set up a state on the Volga called the *Golden Horde*... and turned the Russian princes into their tax collectors.
N: If you wanted to rule a Russian city, you had to travel thousands of kilometres to the Khan, bow, bring gifts... and ask for a *permission slip*.

[SKIT: a Mongol tent, Batu on a carpet, a Russian prince kneeling with a gift box]
PRINCE: [nervous] Great Khan, I humbly ask to rule my little city.
KHAN: [bored] Did you bring the tax?
PRINCE: Every last coin.
KHAN: And the extra tax?
PRINCE: [pause] There's an *extra* tax?
KHAN: [smiling] There is now.

N: This lasted about *two hundred and forty years*. Russians call it the *Mongol yoke*.
N: And here's the twist. One small, unimportant town got very good at this game: *Moscow*.
N: Its prince, *Ivan the First*, collected the Khan's taxes so well that people called him *Ivan Kalita*. *Ivan Moneybag*.
TRAVELLER: [holding up a coin pouch] Great nickname. Terrible for your popularity.
N: Moscow kept a slice of the money, bought up land, and slowly swallowed its neighbours. The head of the Church even moved there.
N: Moscow had learned the Mongols' best lesson: *one ruler, total control, and everyone pays*.
N: In *thirteen eighty*, a Moscow prince, *Dmitry Donskoy*, beat a Mongol army at *Kulikovo Field*. The Horde came back two years later and burned Moscow anyway.
TRAVELLER: [sighing] One step forward, one city on fire.
N: The real end came in *fourteen eighty*, under *Ivan the Third*. A Mongol army and a Moscow army met on opposite banks of the *Ugra River*... and stared at each other. For weeks.
N: Then the Mongols turned around and went home. No great battle. The yoke just... ended.
TRAVELLER: [shrugging] History's most awkward staring contest.
N: Ivan the Third tripled Moscow's land. He married *Sophia*, niece of the last emperor of Constantinople, and took the empire's symbol: the *double-headed eagle*.
N: Moscow now called itself the *Third Rome*. Chaos had produced... total control. Again.

# CHAPTER 3: IVAN THE TERRIBLE

N: *Ivan the Fourth* became ruler of Moscow when he was *three years old*.
N: When he was seven, his mother died suddenly. Many believed she was poisoned.
N: For years, rival noble families, the *boyars*, fought over the boy and the palace. Ivan later wrote that they ignored him, stole from the treasury, and sometimes didn't even feed him.
TRAVELLER: [grim] This is not going to end well.
N: In *fifteen forty-seven*, at sixteen, Ivan did something new. He had himself crowned *Tsar*. From the Roman word *Caesar*.
N: And at first, he was... actually good. New laws, a standing army, and big wins: he conquered the Tatar cities of *Kazan* and *Astrakhan*, opening the whole *Volga* to Moscow.
N: To celebrate Kazan, he built the famous colourful cathedral on Red Square: *Saint Basil's*.
TRAVELLER: [pointing at the domes] That one. The one that looks like a bowl of ice cream.
N: Then, in *fifteen sixty*, his beloved wife *Anastasia* died. Ivan was sure she had been poisoned. And something in him broke.
N: He created the *Oprichnina*: a private police force dressed in black, riding with a dog's head and a broom on their saddles.
TRAVELLER: [whispering] The dog's head meant "we sniff out traitors". The broom meant "we sweep them away".
N: They terrorised the country. In *fifteen seventy*, Ivan's men massacred thousands in the city of *Novgorod*, on suspicion of treason.
N: Then came the worst moment of all. In *fifteen eighty-one*, during a furious argument, Ivan struck his own son and heir with his iron-tipped staff.
N: The young man died days later. Ivan never recovered from it.

[SKIT: the Kremlin at night, a servant tiptoeing past Ivan, who sits alone on his throne]
SERVANT: [timid] Your Majesty... the ambassador from England is here.
IVAN: [growling] Is he loyal?
SERVANT: He's... English, sire.
IVAN: [squinting] Suspicious.

N: When Ivan died in *fifteen eighty-four*, Russia was bigger than ever. Even *Siberia* had begun to open, thanks to a Cossack adventurer called *Yermak*.
N: But the country was exhausted, terrified, and broke. And the next tsar, Ivan's son *Feodor*, was gentle, pious... and had no children.
TRAVELLER: [checking his watch] Total control went too far. You know what comes next.

# CHAPTER 4: THE TIME OF TROUBLES

N: Feodor died in *fifteen ninety-eight*. And with him, the family of Rurik, after more than seven hundred years, was gone.
N: Russians literally named the next fifteen years the *Time of Troubles*.
N: First, a powerful noble called *Boris Godunov* grabbed the throne. Then the weather turned against him.
N: Around *sixteen hundred and one*, frosts and rain wrecked the harvest three years in a row. A terrible famine killed a huge part of the population.
N: And then a young man appeared in Poland with an incredible claim.

[SKIT: a Polish palace hall, a young man bowing to a Polish nobleman]
DMITRY: [grandly] I am *Dmitry*, the lost son of Ivan the Terrible!
NOBLE: [doubtful] Didn't Dmitry die as a child?
DMITRY: [confidently] I got better.
NOBLE: [shrugging] Good enough. Here's an army.

N: The real Dmitry had died in *fifteen ninety-one*. But the *False Dmitry* marched on Moscow with Polish support, and when Boris suddenly died, Moscow let him in. He was crowned tsar.
N: Within a year, angry boyars killed him. The story goes that his body was burned, and his ashes were fired from a cannon back towards Poland.
TRAVELLER: [covering his eyes] That's one way to return to sender.
N: Then came another tsar. Then a *second* False Dmitry. Then the Swedes invaded. Then, in *sixteen ten*, the Polish army marched into Moscow and took the *Kremlin* itself.
N: Russia had no tsar, no government, and a foreign army in its heart.
N: And then, something unusual happened. Ordinary people fixed it.
N: In the city of *Nizhny Novgorod*, a meat trader named *Kuzma Minin* started collecting money for a people's army, and asked a wounded prince, *Dmitry Pozharsky*, to lead it.
N: In *sixteen twelve*, they drove the Poles out of Moscow. Russia still celebrates that day as a national holiday.
TRAVELLER: [impressed] A butcher saving the country. Respect.
N: In *sixteen thirteen*, an assembly chose a new tsar: a shy sixteen-year-old named *Michael Romanov*.
N: His family would rule Russia for the next *three hundred and four years*.
N: Chaos, again, had produced order. The loop was complete. Time for the next one.

# CHAPTER 5: PETER THE GREAT

N: *Peter* was born in *sixteen seventy-two*.
N: At ten, he became co-tsar with his half-brother, while his older sister *Sophia* actually ran things.
N: That same year, rebel palace guards, the *Streltsy*, stormed the Kremlin, and young Peter watched them kill members of his mother's family in front of him.
N: Peter grew up outside the palace, playing war games with real soldiers and real cannons. He grew to over *two metres* tall. Around *six foot eight*.
TRAVELLER: [looking up, way up] He had to duck through most doorways in Russia.
N: In *sixteen ninety-seven*, he did something no tsar had ever done. He went to Western Europe himself, in disguise, as "*Peter Mikhailov*".
N: He worked as a carpenter in Dutch shipyards, studied English factories, and fell in love with everything modern.

[SKIT: a Dutch shipyard, a very tall man in workman's clothes, a Dutch foreman with a clipboard]
FOREMAN: [squinting up] Name?
PETER: [casually] Peter. Just a regular carpenter.
FOREMAN: You're seven feet tall and fifty men follow you everywhere.
PETER: [pause] ...Very regular.

N: Back home, he started changing Russia by force. He ordered his nobles to shave their *beards*. He cut some off himself, with scissors.
N: If you really wanted to keep your beard, you paid a *beard tax*, and carried a token to prove it.
TRAVELLER: [stroking his chin] Imagine needing a licence for your face.
N: European clothes. A new calendar, with New Year on *January the first*. Schools for maths and navigation. A real navy.
N: He fought Sweden for twenty-one years. He lost badly at *Narva* in seventeen hundred... then smashed the Swedes at *Poltava* in *seventeen oh nine*.
N: Now Russia had what it always wanted: a way to the *Baltic Sea*.
N: And on a swamp by that sea, Peter built a brand new capital: *Saint Petersburg*. Thousands of forced labourers died building it.
N: He made himself *Emperor*. Russia was now an *empire*.
N: But Peter's total control reached his own family. His son *Alexei* hated the reforms and fled abroad.
N: Peter lured him home with promises, then had him put on trial. Alexei died in prison in *seventeen eighteen*.
TRAVELLER: [quietly] That's the second tsar in this story to lose his son by his own hand.
N: Peter then made a new law: the emperor could pick any heir he liked. And in *seventeen twenty-five*, he died... without picking anyone.
TRAVELLER: [facepalm] He had one job.

# CHAPTER 6: CATHERINE THE GREAT

N: For the next thirty-seven years, Russia had a strange system: whoever the *palace guards* liked, became ruler.
N: Empresses, a child emperor, regents: one palace coup after another.
N: In *seventeen forty-four*, a teenage German princess named *Sophie* arrived in Russia to marry the heir to the throne, *Peter*.
N: She learned Russian. She converted to Orthodoxy. She took a new name: *Catherine*.
N: Her husband, meanwhile, preferred playing with toy soldiers and openly worshipped Russia's enemy, the King of *Prussia*.
N: In *seventeen sixty-two*, he became *Peter the Third*, and immediately handed back everything Russia had won from Prussia in a long, bloody war.
TRAVELLER: [stunned] He won the game, then gave the other team the trophy.

[SKIT: a palace room, Catherine at a desk, two tall guards officers by the door]
OFFICER: Your Majesty, the guards are... unhappy with your husband.
CATHERINE: [calmly] How unhappy?
OFFICER: About ten thousand soldiers unhappy.
CATHERINE: [standing, smiling] Then I suppose I should get dressed.

N: Six months into his reign, Catherine and the guards overthrew him. A week later, Peter was dead. The official cause, the story goes, was a "*stomach problem*".
TRAVELLER: [raising an eyebrow] Very convenient stomach.
N: *Catherine the Second* ruled for thirty-four years. She wrote to famous French thinkers like *Voltaire*, collected thousands of paintings, which became the *Hermitage* museum, and talked about freedom and reason.
N: She also grabbed a lot of land. *Crimea* in *seventeen eighty-three*. Big parts of *Poland*, which was carved up until it disappeared from the map.
N: And her traders crossed the Pacific. In *seventeen eighty-four*, Russians built their first permanent settlement in a faraway place called... *Alaska*.
TRAVELLER: [checking his watch, grinning] Ooh. Remember that one. We know how this ends.
N: But for most Russians, life got harder. Millions of peasants were *serfs*, owned by landlords, bought and sold with the land.
N: In *seventeen seventy-three*, a Cossack called *Pugachev* claimed to be the dead Peter the Third, and led a huge peasant uprising across the Volga. It took two years to crush.
N: Freedom for the nobles, control for everyone else. The loop, in a powdered wig.
TRAVELLER: [to camera] By the way, if you're enjoying this, subscribe. It's free, and it makes the time machine run.
N: When Catherine died in *seventeen ninety-six*, Russia was a giant on Europe's stage.
N: And in the west, a short French general was about to test exactly how big that giant really was.
