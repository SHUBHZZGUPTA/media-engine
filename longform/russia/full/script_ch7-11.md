# Russia — script, chapters 7–11 + ending (second half)

Same format as before: `N:` narrator · `TRAVELLER:` mascot · other names are skit characters. `*word*` = keyword caption. `[tag]` = delivery.
Plot follows Ziro's second half beat for beat; skits and jokes are ours. Living people (Putin) are shown only through places, maps and news-style archive, never as a character, and the Today chapter states facts and what each side says.

---

# CHAPTER 7: NAPOLEON

N: By *eighteen twelve*, *Napoleon Bonaparte* ran most of Europe.
N: Russia's new tsar, *Alexander the First*, stopped playing by Napoleon's rules. So Napoleon invaded, with the biggest army Europe had ever seen: around *six hundred thousand* men.
TRAVELLER: [whistling] That's not an army. That's a country on a walking holiday.
N: And the Russians did something infuriating. They kept *retreating*. And as they went, they burned their own crops and villages, so the French found nothing to eat.
N: When the Russians finally stood and fought, at *Borodino*, around *seventy thousand* men were killed or wounded in a single day.
N: Then the Russians pulled back again... and Napoleon marched into *Moscow*.
N: He expected the tsar to beg for peace. Instead, he found an almost empty city. And that night, fires broke out everywhere. Most of Moscow *burned*.

[SKIT: a burned Moscow palace room, Napoleon pacing, a French aide holding a stack of letters]
NAPOLEON: [impatient] Has the tsar answered?
AIDE: No, sire.
NAPOLEON: Then send him another letter. A nicer one.
AIDE: [reading] Sire, this one has a little drawing of us shaking hands.
NAPOLEON: [pause] ...Add a heart.

N: Alexander never answered. After five weeks in a burned city, Napoleon gave up and turned for home.
N: And then came Russia's oldest ally: *winter*.
N: Starving, freezing soldiers collapsed in the snow, while *Cossacks* picked off anyone who fell behind. Only a small fraction of that giant army made it out.
TRAVELLER: [shivering] Rule number one of invading Russia: don't.
N: The Russians chased Napoleon across Europe, and in *eighteen fourteen*, Russian soldiers paraded through *Paris*.
N: But those officers had seen something in the West. Constitutions. Laws that even kings had to follow.
N: In *December eighteen twenty-five*, a group of them marched their troops into a square in Saint Petersburg to demand change. The *Decembrists*.
N: The new tsar, *Nicholas the First*, answered with cannons. Five leaders were hanged. Over a hundred were sent to *Siberia*.
N: Then Russia lost the *Crimean War*, on its own doorstep, against Britain, France and the Ottomans.
N: That shock finally forced change. In *eighteen sixty-one*, *Alexander the Second* freed around *twenty-three million serfs*. Two years *before* Abraham Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation.
N: And Russia needed money. It couldn't really defend a faraway colony across the ocean anyway. So in *eighteen sixty-seven*, Alexander sold *Alaska* to the United States for *seven point two million dollars*.
TRAVELLER: [tapping his watch, grinning] And there it is. The garage sale from the start of the video.
N: Some Americans laughed and called it *Seward's Folly*, after the man who bought it. Then came the gold. Then the oil. Nobody's laughing now.
N: Alexander reformed the courts, the army, local government. And on *March the thirteenth, eighteen eighty-one*, he approved a plan that could have been Russia's first step towards an elected assembly.
N: That same afternoon, revolutionaries threw a bomb at his carriage. It missed. Alexander stepped out to check on the wounded... and a second bomber threw another, right at his feet.
TRAVELLER: [quietly] The tsar who freed the serfs. Killed the day he tried to give people a voice.
N: His son tore up the plan. The loop was back on its old setting. And the tsar after that... would be the last one.

# CHAPTER 8: THE LAST TSAR

N: *Nicholas the Second* became tsar in *eighteen ninety-four*.
N: He loved his wife, *Alexandra*, adored his five children, and by most accounts was a polite, gentle man.
N: He was also completely unprepared to run the biggest country on Earth.
TRAVELLER: [diplomatically] Lovely guy. Would not let him look after my goldfish.
N: In *nineteen oh four*, Russia went to war with *Japan*, expecting an easy win. So it sent its *Baltic Fleet* all the way around the world.
N: It went wrong almost immediately. In the North Sea, nervous Russian sailors mistook *British fishing boats* for Japanese torpedo boats... and opened fire.

[SKIT: a foggy ship's deck at night, an admiral with a telescope, a young sailor]
SAILOR: [panicking] Admiral! Enemy ships!
ADMIRAL: [peering] Japanese?
SAILOR: Definitely! They're... they're holding fishing nets, sir.
ADMIRAL: [lowering the telescope] Sailor. Where are we?
SAILOR: [small voice] ...Near England, sir.

N: That really happened. Russia nearly started a war with Britain by accident.
N: When the fleet finally reached Japan, after *eight months* at sea, the Japanese navy destroyed it at *Tsushima* in about *two days*.
N: An Asian power had crushed a European empire in a modern war. Back home, people were furious.
N: In *January nineteen oh five*, thousands of peaceful workers marched to the tsar's *Winter Palace*, carrying icons and portraits of Nicholas, just to hand him a petition.
N: Soldiers opened fire. Hundreds were killed. It became known as *Bloody Sunday*.
N: And it killed something else that day: the idea that the tsar was the people's loving father.
N: Then, in *nineteen fourteen*, Russia joined *World War One*.
N: The front stretched from the Baltic Sea almost to the Black Sea. Within weeks, a whole Russian army was surrounded and wiped out at *Tannenberg*.
N: Soldiers went into battle without enough rifles, boots, or bullets. Millions were killed, wounded, or captured.
N: In *nineteen fifteen*, Nicholas went to the front to lead the army himself, leaving Alexandra in charge at home.
N: And Alexandra trusted one man above everyone: *Grigori Rasputin*. A wild, wandering holy man from Siberia, who seemed able to help her son *Alexei*, who had *haemophilia*: his bleeding wouldn't stop.
N: To Alexandra, Rasputin was a miracle. To everyone else, he was a scandal. Ministers were being hired and fired because he said so.
N: In *December nineteen sixteen*, a group of nobles decided to kill him.
N: The famous version says poisoned cakes didn't work, bullets didn't work, and he was finally thrown into an icy river.
TRAVELLER: [raising a finger] Historians think the killers exaggerated a lot of that... to sound like heroes.
N: Either way, Rasputin was gone. And the monarchy had only weeks left.
N: In *February nineteen seventeen*, women queuing for bread that never came started a riot in the capital. The riot became a *revolution*.
N: Nicholas ordered the soldiers to shoot. This time, they joined the crowds instead.
N: Days later, in his royal train, Nicholas signed away his throne. *Three hundred and four years* of Romanov rule, ended with one signature.
N: The new government then made one fatal mistake. It kept fighting the war.
N: And a bald, furious revolutionary named *Vladimir Lenin* promised Russians exactly what they wanted: *Peace. Land. Bread.*

[SKIT: a smoky back room, Lenin at a table, a nervous party comrade with a notebook]
COMRADE: Comrade Lenin, our slogan. "Peace, land, bread"?
LENIN: [nodding] Simple. Strong.
COMRADE: And when do we deliver the peace?
LENIN: [pause] After the civil war.
COMRADE: [writing slowly] ...Peace, after war. Got it.

N: In *October nineteen seventeen*, Lenin's *Bolsheviks* seized power.
N: Then came a brutal *civil war*: Lenin's *Reds* against the anti-communist *Whites*. Britain, France, America and Japan all sent troops. Millions died from fighting, hunger and disease.
N: In *July nineteen eighteen*, in a cellar in the city of *Yekaterinburg*, the Bolsheviks shot Nicholas, Alexandra, their four daughters, their son Alexei, and their servants.
N: The Reds won. And in *nineteen twenty-two*, the old empire became a brand new country: the *Union of Soviet Socialist Republics*. The *USSR*.
TRAVELLER: [checking his watch] Collapse... then a new man promises order. You know what comes next.

# CHAPTER 9: STALIN

N: Lenin died in *nineteen twenty-four*.
N: Scientists sliced his brain into *thousands* of thin pieces, looking for the secret of his genius. His body was preserved and put on display in Red Square, where it still lies today.
TRAVELLER: [grimacing] They didn't find the genius. They did find a lot of brain.
N: Before he died, Lenin had warned the party: *Joseph Stalin* was too rude and too powerful, and should be removed.
N: The party ignored him.
N: By the end of the nineteen-twenties, Stalin had outplayed every rival. The biggest, *Leon Trotsky*, was thrown out of the country, hunted down in *Mexico*... and killed with an *ice axe*.
N: Stalin wanted to turn a country of peasants into an industrial superpower in about ten years.
N: So he forced farmers off their own land and onto giant state farms. Those who resisted were shot, or sent to labour camps: the *Gulag*.
N: Grain was taken away even as people starved. In *nineteen thirty-two and thirty-three*, millions died in a famine. *Ukraine* suffered the most. Ukrainians call it the *Holodomor*: death by hunger.
N: Then Stalin turned on everyone else. In *nineteen thirty-seven and thirty-eight*, his secret police arrested around *one and a half million* people, and shot around *seven hundred thousand* of them.
N: Generals. Scientists. Poets. Ordinary people, reported by their own neighbours.
N: And some didn't just disappear from life. They disappeared from *photos*.

[SKIT: a Soviet photo lab, a nervous photo editor with a retouching brush, a stern official]
OFFICIAL: [holding up a photo] Comrade. Who is this man next to Comrade Stalin?
EDITOR: [quickly] What man?
OFFICIAL: That one. Right there.
EDITOR: [brushing furiously] I see only Comrade Stalin. And a very beautiful canal.
OFFICIAL: [nodding slowly] ...Good answer.

N: That man was *Nikolai Yezhov*, the secret police boss who had run the killings. After he was shot himself, he was painted out of the photo, leaving Stalin alone by the water.
TRAVELLER: [pointing back] Remember the dictator who erased people from photos? That's him.
N: In *nineteen thirty-nine*, Stalin shocked the world. He signed a deal with his sworn enemy, *Adolf Hitler*. They promised not to attack each other, and secretly split Eastern Europe between them.
N: Stalin trusted it. His spies warned him, again and again, that Germany was coming. He ignored them.
N: On *June the twenty-second, nineteen forty-one*, more than *three million* German and allied soldiers invaded. Three giant pushes: towards *Leningrad*, towards *Moscow*, and into the south.
N: The largest invasion in human history. And the Red Army, gutted by Stalin's own purges, collapsed.
N: *Leningrad*, Peter the Great's city, was surrounded for nearly *nine hundred days*. People boiled leather belts and wallpaper paste just to have something to eat. Around *a million* civilians died.
N: The Germans reached the edge of Moscow. And then... winter arrived. Again.
TRAVELLER: [to camera] Rule number one. Still: don't.
N: Then came *Stalingrad*: the city named after Stalin himself. For months, the two armies fought street by street, sometimes room by room.
N: In the end, a whole German army was surrounded and forced to surrender. After that, Germany never stopped going backwards.
N: In *nineteen forty-five*, Soviet soldiers raised the red flag over *Berlin*.
N: The cost is almost impossible to imagine. Around *twenty-seven million* Soviet people died. Almost every family lost someone.
N: Stalin came out of the war ruling half of Europe. By *nineteen forty-nine*, he had the *atomic bomb*.
N: And in *nineteen fifty-three*, his death might have been the most Stalin thing of all.

[SKIT: a dark corridor outside a closed door, two guards whispering]
GUARD ONE: [whispering] He hasn't come out all day.
GUARD TWO: So knock.
GUARD ONE: You knock. The last man who woke him up is in Siberia.
GUARD TWO: [pause] ...Let's give him one more hour.

N: When they finally went in, he was lying on the floor. And good doctors were hard to find, because many of the country's top doctors had just been arrested, in a plot Stalin himself had invented.
N: He died a few days later. The man who terrified a whole country died, at least partly, because everyone was too terrified to help him.

# CHAPTER 10: THE COLD WAR

N: After the war, the USSR and America spent decades staring each other down: the *Cold War*.
N: The world split in two. Red on one side, blue on the other... with *thousands* of nuclear weapons pointed across the middle.
N: At first, the Soviets were winning the races that mattered. In *nineteen fifty-seven*, they launched the first satellite: *Sputnik*.
N: In *nineteen sixty-one*, *Yuri Gagarin* became the first human in space.
TRAVELLER: [looking up, amazed] From Viking longboats... to orbit.
N: Then, in *nineteen sixty-two*, the Soviets secretly put nuclear missiles in *Cuba*, about *ninety miles* from Florida.
N: For *thirteen days*, the world sat on the edge of nuclear war. And the scariest moment happened underwater.
N: A Soviet submarine near Cuba was being hunted by American ships, dropping small practice charges to force it up. The crew had lost contact with Moscow. They thought the war might already have started.
N: And they had a *nuclear torpedo*.

[SKIT: a cramped red-lit submarine, a sweating captain, the calm officer Arkhipov]
CAPTAIN: [shouting] They're bombing us! The war has started! Prepare the torpedo!
ARKHIPOV: [calmly] Or... they're knocking.
CAPTAIN: Knocking?!
ARKHIPOV: They want us to come up. So... we come up.

N: According to the crew, firing needed all three senior officers to agree. One of them, *Vasily Arkhipov*, said no. The submarine surfaced.
TRAVELLER: [quietly] One man said no. And we're all still here.
N: After that, the USSR slowly ran out of steam. It could build giant rockets, but it couldn't keep shoes in its shops.
N: People queued for hours, sometimes without knowing what was at the front of the queue.
N: In *nineteen eighty-five*, a younger leader, *Mikhail Gorbachev*, tried to fix it by letting people speak more freely: *glasnost*.
N: A year later, a reactor exploded at *Chernobyl*. The government's first instinct was to hide it.
N: The world found out when radiation alarms went off at a power plant in *Sweden*, more than a thousand kilometres away.
N: And once people could speak openly, they had a lot to say.
N: In *nineteen eighty-nine*, Eastern Europe broke free, and the *Berlin Wall* came down.
N: In *August nineteen ninety-one*, hardliners tried to seize power. Russia's elected leader, *Boris Yeltsin*, climbed onto a tank in Moscow and told people to resist.
N: The coup collapsed in three days. And so did the USSR.
N: On *December the twenty-fifth, nineteen ninety-one*, the red flag came down over the Kremlin for the last time. One giant country broke into *fifteen*.

# CHAPTER 11: TODAY

N: The nineteen-nineties were brutal. People's savings vanished.
N: A handful of well-connected men bought up oil and gas companies for almost nothing, and became *billionaires* almost overnight.
N: Teachers and soldiers went months without pay.
N: And Gorbachev, the last leader of a superpower? In the late nineties, he appeared in a *Pizza Hut* advert.
TRAVELLER: [blinking] From the nuclear button... to stuffed crust.
N: *Chaos*. And by now, you know what comes after chaos in Russia.
N: On *New Year's Eve, nineteen ninety-nine*, Yeltsin resigned and handed power to a little-known former *KGB* officer: *Vladimir Putin*.
N: Exhausted Russians welcomed his promise of order. Supporters credit him with bringing back stability, and incomes rose fast in the two-thousands, helped by high oil prices.
N: Critics point to what came with it. Step by step, control tightened. Independent TV channels were taken over. Opposition leaders were jailed, exiled, or worse.
N: In *twenty fourteen*, Russia annexed *Crimea*.
N: In *twenty twenty*, the constitution was changed so Putin could stay in power until *twenty thirty-six*.
N: And in *February twenty twenty-two*, Russia launched a *full-scale invasion* of *Ukraine*: the biggest war in Europe since World War Two.
N: As this video was made, that war is still going on. Peace talks keep starting and stopping. And Putin is still in the Kremlin.

# ENDING

N: So look back at the whole story.
N: The Slavs invited the *Vikings*, because of chaos.
N: Moscow learned total control from the *Mongols*.
N: The *Time of Troubles* gave Russia the *Romanovs*.
N: The fall of the tsars gave Russia *Lenin* and *Stalin*.
N: And the chaos of the nineties gave Russia *Putin*.
N: *Eleven hundred years*. Different hats. *Same story*.
TRAVELLER: [looking at his watch, then at the camera] Will Russia ever break the loop?
N: Nobody knows. Maybe not even the Russians.
TRAVELLER: [grinning, winding his watch] And if you thought Russia's rulers were rough on their own families... wait until you meet the *Ottomans*. Their sultans had a law that let them kill their own brothers. See you there.
