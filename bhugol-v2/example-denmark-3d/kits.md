Description:
Denmark has a desert: Råbjerg Mile, a moving sand dune near Skagen that walks up to 18 m a year.
Why rainy Denmark grew a desert, and why it kept this one on purpose.

Danes cleared their forests for timber and farmland, and by about 1800 only 2–4% of Denmark was forest. With nothing holding it down, the wind drove sand up to 7 km inland, burying farms and villages. It reached Skagen's church, the region's biggest; people dug the door free for about 20 years until it closed in 1795, and only the tower still stands. Denmark fought back with a 1792 sand-drift decree and planted forests, and by the 1950s the drift was under control. But the state bought Råbjerg Mile in 1900 and lets it roam free. The story goes the sand that buried the church came from this very dune. In a century or two it should reach the main road to Skagen.
