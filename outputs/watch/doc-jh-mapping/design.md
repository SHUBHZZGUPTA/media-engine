### 6. THE 10 MOST REUSABLE DESIGN RULES

1. **Combine High-Tech & Tactile Textures:** Blend modern satellite imagery with physical paper maps, handwritten Sharpie notes, tape, and pushpins.
2. **Use Highlight Badges for Key Phrases:** Place critical map names or stats inside bright yellow (`#FFD700`) highlighter rectangles behind black text for instant eye tracking.
3. **Layer Paper Cutouts over Animated Backgrounds:** Stop-motion style historical character cutouts placed on top of looping 2D animated ocean waves create an engaging, storybook feel.
4. **Anchor Text with High-Contrast Box Badges:** Put secondary location tags inside solid black pills or boxes with white uppercase text to guarantee readability over complex map terrain.
5. **Establish a Dedicated Investigative Board:** Use a corkboard with red string connections, pinned document scans, and sticky notes as a recurring visual hub for complex historical narrative threads.
6. **Blend Historical Maps directly onto Satellite GIS Data:** Overlay semi-transparent vintage cartography onto real-world satellite views to illustrate geographic inaccuracies.
7. **Pair Text Animations with Tactile Sound Design:** Every text pop, marker stroke, paper drop, and map fold should have a corresponding crisp sound effect (paper tear, marker squeak, thud).
8. **Keep Map Camera Moves in Continuous Motion:** Always maintain a slow pan, subtle zoom, or drift on map graphics to prevent static visual plateaus.
9. **Use Selective Color Hierarchy in Graphs:** Draw focus on data charts by rendering the target metric in a bold primary color (e.g., thick black line) while background comparison data remains thin and muted grey.
10. **Limit Font Families:** Stick to a clean two-font system—a classic editorial serif for major historical titles/quotes and a clean condensed sans-serif for map callouts, stats, and UI badges.
