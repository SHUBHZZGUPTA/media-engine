### 1. On-Screen Text Layout

* **Map & Data Callout Labels**  
  * **Timestamp:** `03:12` – `03:34` (`SAHARA`, `SAHEL`, `Sudanian Savanna`), `04:58` (`LAKE CHAD`), `05:54` (`SENGAL`, `DJIBOUTI`), `08:08` (`MALI`, `BURKINA FASO`, `NIGER`).
  * **Position & Size:** Center of regions or pinned directly above geographical features. Heights range from 3% to 6% of frame height.
  * **Font Style & Color:** All-caps, bold sans-serif (`Montserrat` / `Helvetica Bold`). White text centered inside filled rectangular pills.
  * **Background Pill:** Solid orange-red (`#E85A3C` / `#D84A2A`) or deep grey boxes with rounded corners.
  * **Animation:** Scale-up / pop-in with a slight elastic bounce (~0.2s duration), remaining static on screen for 3–8 seconds before wiping off or fading out with the map transition.
* **Large Stat Callouts**  
  * **Timestamp:** `02:14` (`2,700,000,000 PEOPLE`), `03:18` (`9,200,000,000 KM²`), `06:18` (`4,800 Miles`), `06:34` (`$8,000,000,000`).
  * **Position & Size:** Lower-third or centered over the continent/map, occupying 8%–14% of frame height.
  * **Font Style & Color:** Heavy, bold sans-serif with tracked-out spacing. Primary numbers are pure white (`#FFFFFF`); secondary unit descriptors are slightly smaller below or beside.
  * **Background:** Drop shadow against a dark desaturated map background; no solid box container to maximize map visibility.
* **Stacked Text & Point Lists**  
  * **Timestamp:** `02:25` (`30 Years` vs `1 Year`), `03:00` (`ANTARCTICA` / `ARCTIC` / `AFRICA`).
  * **Method:** Words are stacked vertically inside matching red-orange rectangle tags (`03:00`) or placed beneath identical graphical icons (`02:25` hourglasses).
  * **Clutter Prevention:** The channel avoids complex multi-sentence paragraphs. Text is strictly limited to **1–4 words per callout**, letting the voiceover convey detailed explanations while the visual acts as an anchor.

### 2. Time / Era Indicators

* **Year Comparison Tags (Historical Maps)**  
  * **Timestamp:** `05:00` (Lake Chad inset comparing `1973` vs `2017`).
  * **Design & Position:** Top-left corner inset cards; small, white sans-serif text inside black semi-transparent pills (`#000000` with 70% opacity) positioned at the bottom of split-screen satellite thumbnails.
  * **Animation:** Static overlay on the inset image cards; pop-in scale entry.
* **Graphical Duration Comparisons**  
  * **Timestamp:** `02:25` – `02:30`.
  * **Design:** Two identical vector hourglasses side-by-side. Sand flows inside both simultaneously, but the `1 Year` timer fills and empties 30× faster than the `30 Years` timer to visually demonstrate acceleration.
  * **Voice Sync:** Exact frame alignment—the `30 Years` tag appears when the voiceover says "What used to take 30 years," and the `1 Year` tag appears on "is now happening in just a single year."

  * **Basemap:** Dark gray-blue desaturated satellite image (`#1E252B` landmasses, `#13191E` oceans).

* **Water & River Animation:**  
  * **Timestamp:** `04:58` – `05:25` (Lake Chad contraction).
  * **Animation:** Blue vector polygons shrink over time from the historical boundary down to <10% fill. Satellite picture insets swap between 1973 (lush blue/green) and 2017 (pale green/dry lake bed).
* **Label Placement & Camera Motion:**  
  * **Labels:** Clean, horizontal orientation; vector pins connect red pills to specific geographic points.
  * **Camera Moves:** Constant smooth virtual camera movement—slow zoom-ins (`02:54` to `03:12` into North Africa), linear pans across latitude lines (`05:46` westward along the Sahel corridor), and scale snaps to contextualize distance (`06:26` sliding the wall outline up to Europe/Asia from Portugal to Mongolia).

### 5. Chapter / Section Transitions

* **Visual Continuity:**  
  * Rather than hard cuts or wipe transitions, chapters shift spatially using the map camera.
  * **Example (`02:54`):** The world map zooms into the African continent, the background darkens further, and the Sahara desert layer highlights in orange, signaling the shift from global desertification to the specific case study of North Africa.
  * When transitioning from map graphics to real-world footage (`04:28`), a quick 4-frame cross-dissolve is paired with ambient field audio or sound effects.

### 6. The 10 Most Reusable Design Rules

1. **Use a Dark Desaturated Basemap:** Keep continents dark grey/blue so bright vector shapes (orange deserts, green forests) pop with high contrast.
2. **Standardize Label Pills:** Enclose region names in rounded rectangular pills with bold, all-caps sans-serif text for instant legibility over complex satellite maps.
3. **Keep On-Screen Text Minimal:** Never display full sentences on map callouts; limit text to 1–4 words per box to complement the voiceover.
4. **Use Motion Camera Panning for Scale:** Slowly pan the camera along distance measurement lines (e.g., across 4,800 miles of the Sahel) to physically demonstrate scale.
5. **Animate Numbers with Unit Tags:** Pair large metric values (e.g., `$8,000,000,000`) with smaller secondary descriptive text beneath them.
6. **Represent Populations with Icon Lines:** Use rows of simple white human silhouettes to visualize population growth rather than abstract bar graphs.
7. **Use Geographic Comparisons for Context:** Overlay an abstract shape onto a familiar continent (e.g., comparing the Green Wall length to the distance between Portugal and Mongolia) to help viewers grasp scale.
8. **Animate Charts In-Place:** Use clockwise-filling circular pie charts and vector percentage fills directly over map locations.
9. **Use Dynamic Dashed Measurement Lines:** Draw dashed lines from point A to point B with dual-ended tick marks to show distances and widths.
10. **Maintain Spatial Transitions:** Use map camera zooms and pans to bridge topic shifts instead of generic video transitions like wipes or spins.
