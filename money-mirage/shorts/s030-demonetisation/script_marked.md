# SHORT: THE NIGHT INDIA CANCELLED ITS CASH
N: Ten years ago this week, India cancelled about *86%* of its cash in *four hours.* The target was *black money.* So how much did it catch?
N: On *8 November 2016,* the government announced that 500 and 1,000-rupee notes would stop being legal tender at *midnight.*
N: The idea: hidden, untaxed cash couldn't be deposited without questions, so it would simply *die.*
GOLDIE: [smug, deep, rich] Hidden cash? Me? Never.
N: Then came weeks of bank *queues.*
N: In 2018, the central bank finished counting the old notes. *99.3%* had come back.
N: Out of every hundred rupees cancelled, about *ninety-nine* returned to the banks.
N: Critics say most black money was never sitting in cash; it was in property, gold, or slipped back in. The government pointed to more digital payments and more taxpayers.
N: That's the *mirage:* banning the *notes* doesn't ban the *wealth* behind them.
