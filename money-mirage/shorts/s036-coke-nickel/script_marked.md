# SHORT: THE 5-CENT COKE
N: Could you hold one price for *70 years?* Coca-Cola did. A Coke cost *5 cents* through two world wars. Why couldn't it *rise?*
N: In *1899,* two lawyers got Coke's bottling rights, with the *syrup* price fixed *permanently.*
N: Not exactly a *great* contract.
N: So why not just charge *more?*
N: Coke tied its brand to *five cents,* and its vending machines took a *single* nickel. The next coin up was a *dime,* double the price.
N: And that's not even the *strangest* part.
N: Coke asked the US Treasury for a *7½-cent* coin. Its boss even asked his hunting buddy, President *Eisenhower.* The answer was *no.*
N: Inflation finally won. By *1959,* the nickel Coke was *gone.*
N: The price never moved. Your *nickel* just kept getting *weaker.*
