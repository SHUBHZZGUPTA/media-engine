# SHORT: CHARLES PONZI, 1920
N: In *1920,* a man in Boston promised you *50%* profit in just *45 days.*
N: His name was *Charles Ponzi.* His idea sounded clever: *postal reply coupons.*
N: Buy them cheap in Europe, cash them in America for more. A real price gap. On *paper.*
N: But you couldn't do it at scale. So he paid old investors with *new* investors' money.
N: Thousands lined up. He took in roughly *$15 million* in about *eight months.*
N: Then the *Boston Post* started digging. By *August 1920,* it was over.
N: Investors lost about *$20 million.* That's over *$200 million* today. The Post won a *Pulitzer.*
N: Ponzi went to prison, was deported to Italy, and died broke in Rio in *1949.*
N: That's the mirage: the early profits were real, because they were *your* money.
N: So when returns are huge and nobody can explain *how,* walk away.
