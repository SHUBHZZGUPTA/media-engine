# SHORT: THE STEAK NOBODY ORDERS
N: Why does a menu have a *$100 steak?* Often, it's not there to be *ordered.*
N: It's there to make the *$45* steak look *cheap.*
N: Psychologists call it *anchoring:* the first number you see bends every number after it.
GOLDIE: [smug, deep, rich] Only forty-five? How generous of me.
N: In a famous 1974 experiment, Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman spun a wheel rigged to stop on *10* or *65.*
N: Then they asked: what share of UN members are *African* countries?
N: People who saw *10* guessed about *25%.* People who saw *65* guessed about *45%.*
N: A random number moved their answer by *twenty points.*
N: Now imagine that number is on a *menu.*
N: That's the *mirage:* the expensive item isn't the product. It's the *ruler.*
N: Next time, ignore the top price and ask: would I pay *this* anywhere else?
BILL: [nervous, squeaky] So I feel rich for buying the second-most expensive thing?
N: Comment: what's the most overpriced thing you've ever ordered?
