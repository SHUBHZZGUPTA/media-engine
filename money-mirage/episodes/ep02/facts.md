## Conflicts and caveats

1. **Time of the 12% rise:** BoE QB 1992 Q4 says 11:00am, and Naef (Cambridge), UnHerd and Econlib agree. The Guardian (2012, via Wikipedia) reportedly says 10:30, but it could not be fetched. Use **11am**.
2. **Time of the 15% rise:** BoE says 2:15pm and Literary Review says 2:30pm. Use **2:15pm** (BoE).
3. **Suspension announcement:** BoE says "just after 7.30pm", Naef says 7:30pm, Econlib says 7:40pm and the Guardian reportedly says 7pm. Use **about 7.30pm**. The decision was communicated to central banks around 4pm.
4. **Reserves spent:** Naef's archive-based "$22bn+ in a few hours" is the best-sourced figure. "£27bn" could not be verified. Do not confuse spend with **loss**: the £3.3bn (≈$4.9bn) is a net loss, not the amount spent. Some secondary sources (NPR, KCL) blur the two.
5. **1997 cost estimate:** £3.4bn, £3.14bn and £3.3bn all appear. None could be checked against a primary 1997 document, so the 1997 figure is **UNVERIFIED**. The 2005 FOI £3.3bn (measured at Feb 1994) is solid.
6. **Soros numbers:** "Almost $10bn" is Soros's own reported estimate, but only via secondary quotes, because the Times and NYT originals were blocked. Fund size varies by source: ~$5bn (NPR/Johnson), ~$7bn (IFR) and "$1bn" (a Stanford student page; disregard). Profit of about $1bn is consistent across Britannica, the NYT wire (via a secondary source) and *Soros on Soros*. "Over £1bn" (Telegraph) is unverified.
7. **"Go for the jugular":** The phrase is attested in Druckenmiller's own words (Schwager, 1994) as a general Soros lesson. Mallaby ties it to the sterling trade (Atlantic excerpt, 2010, read only via Priceonomics). Attribute it on screen as "according to Druckenmiller / Mallaby".
8. **Lira devaluation date:** The deal was struck over the weekend of 12–13 Sep and took effect Mon 14 Sep. NPR's "Friday 11 Sep" and IFR's "7 Sep" are wrong or less authoritative.
9. **30 Sep sterling level:** The BoE text gives a low of DM 2.5005, and its Table A gives a close of DM 2.5095. The difference is low versus close, so label which one is shown.
10. **1997 majority:** The Commons Library says 178 and many outlets say 179. Use 178.
11. **Per-person cost:** About £57 uses the 1992 population with a Feb-1994-measured £3.3bn. Label it as approximate.
12. **Blocked sources:** These could not be fetched in this environment and so are not cited as read: Guardian (2005 FOI article, 2012 timeline), BBC News, Telegraph, NYT, The Atlantic, Independent, Bloomberg, the Wayback Machine, the HM Treasury "Cost of Black Wednesday reconsidered" PDF, and ScienceDirect (Eichengreen & Naef 2022). If the script needs any of their specific figures, re-check them in a browser.
